# Introduction
Selecting elements that jointly satisfy a request is a central problem for language-model agents. In tool selection, relevant operations must compose through their input–output dependencies; identifying individually plausible tools does not by itself recover the required operation set. Tool Graph Complexity reports differences in selection success across dependency patterns beyond what reference-set size alone describes[^toolgraph]. These observations motivate our central question: which structural selection difficulties persist when the selected objects are knowledge-graph entities connected by factual relations?

## Relevance to the Web
Our research question concerns reliable access to interlinked structured knowledge by language-model agents. We use a corporate KG developed for GraphRAG evaluation, connecting companies, people, products, locations, and news[^corporatekg]. We examine whether an agent retains the complete query-defined entity set and rejects irrelevant neighboring entities. This examines a selection step relevant to consuming structured knowledge for grounding and verification, linking the study to The Web Conference's Semantics and Knowledge track. The experiments evaluate selection reliability rather than downstream answer quality.

Knowledge graphs (KGs) provide a setting in which directed relations can be explicitly presented alongside candidate descriptions. This permits examining whether tool-side structural profiles also appear in entity selection and how the KG profiles depend on the evidence supplied to the model. The comparison concerns the recovery of a query-defined entity set, rather than tool execution or parameter transfer between models. Tool dependencies and KG relations have different semantics, so similar profiles would not by themselves establish a shared computational mechanism.

We study an exhaustive selection task. For each query, the selection protocol requires the named anchor, the endpoints, and every intermediate entity on all permitted supporting routes. Selecting only plausible endpoints or one supporting branch does not meet this objective. Every reference entity is included in a fixed candidate catalogue, allowing us to evaluate omissions and excess selection directly. Complete entity-set recovery is the declared selection requirement; it does not imply that each entity's separate card is indispensable evidence for an endpoint answer.

The initial Tool-to-KG comparison already appears in Appendix O of Tool Graph Complexity[^toolgraph]. It reports partial structural transfer, easier explicit-KG chains, residual incoming-tree difficulty, and adjacent-decoy errors. We build on these findings to examine how KG selection profiles change with relation availability and presentation, and which incomplete outputs persist or change on matched tasks. This extends the interpretation of the prior comparison rather than claiming the first KG transfer experiment.

Our analysis uses archived outcomes spanning seven task families and three language models. Raw versus Raw+E adds explicit relations to otherwise matched entity cards. Controlled triples, templated prose, and alternative card hosting retain designated unique facts while changing their presentation; both-card hosting additionally changes repetition and input length. Adjacent-context conditions introduce connected but query-irrelevant candidates. Separating these comparisons matters: supplying previously unavailable relations is different from presenting the same facts differently, and native descriptions are not assumed to contain every reference relation.

We ask three questions.

- **RQ1:** Which structural selection profiles reported for tools are also observed in KG entity selection, and where do the KG task-family and reference-size profiles differ?
- **RQ2:** How do relation availability, matched-fact presentation, and adjacent distractors condition these KG profiles?
- **RQ3:** Which residual output errors remain when relations are explicit, and how do they change on the same tasks under presentation and query-formulation variants?

The Tool-side comparison uses previously reported findings; we do not estimate a new harmonized cross-domain effect. Family comparisons likewise do not isolate topology from query semantics and entity or relation types.

The study contributes an evidence-conditioned assessment of KG selection profiles in relation to prior Tool-to-KG findings. Its additional evidence consists of paired KG relation-availability and matched-fact presentation comparisons, together with localized analyses of incomplete and excessive selections and their transitions. Recall measures coverage of the exhaustive reference, while precision and exact recovery distinguish over-selection and complete-set correctness. The analysis reuses existing tasks and outcomes; exact task/response overlap with Appendix O remains unquantified, and independent replication is not claimed.

The results show that relation disclosure increases coverage, while residual profiles vary across task families and models. On incoming-tree tasks, some high-precision outputs retain only part of the required route set. Presentation variants can correct these outputs but also introduce regressions; a decrease in one error signature need not produce a comparable increase in exact recovery. These findings qualify the interpretation of structural transfer: explicit relations enable stronger recovery in the studied KG conditions, but neither relation visibility nor a shared motif label is a sufficient explanation of complete selection behavior.
