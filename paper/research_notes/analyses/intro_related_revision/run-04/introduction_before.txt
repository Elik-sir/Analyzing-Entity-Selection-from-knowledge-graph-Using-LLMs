\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Language-model systems can search the Web and use retrieved information to answer questions~\cite{nakano2021webgpt}. Questions about related entities may require combining textual descriptions with relational information~\cite{wu2024stark}. For example, a question about a company may involve its products, investors, or related news. In such cases, the model must identify which entities and connections jointly support the requested answer.

Knowledge graphs (KGs) represent such information as entities and directed relations. This motivates a basic selection question: if all required entities and their relations are provided, can an LLM recover the complete set specified by the query? We examine this question in a fixed candidate catalogue containing every reference entity. In the explicit-relation conditions, the links needed to recover that reference are also supplied. Our evaluation focuses on selecting the required entity set as a step toward answering the question.

This problem is closely related to tool selection, where relevant operations must compose through their input--output dependencies. Identifying individually plausible tools does not by itself recover the required operation set. Tool Graph Complexity reports differences in selection success across dependency patterns beyond what reference-set size alone describes~\cite{anonymousToolGraph}. Our central question is which structural selection difficulties observed for tools persist when the selected objects are KG entities connected by factual relations. The different semantics of tool dependencies and KG relations make this a question to investigate, rather than an assumed equivalence.

We study this transfer using a corporate KG developed for GraphRAG evaluation, connecting companies, people, products, locations, and news~\cite{kirpichev2026towards}. For each query, the selection protocol requires the named anchor, the endpoints, and every intermediate entity on all permitted supporting routes. Selecting only plausible endpoints or one supporting branch does not meet this objective. Complete entity-set recovery is the declared selection requirement; it does not imply that each entity's separate card is indispensable evidence for an endpoint answer.

The initial Tool-to-KG comparison already appears in Appendix~O of Tool Graph Complexity~\cite{anonymousToolGraph}. It reports partial structural transfer, easier explicit-KG chains, residual incoming-tree difficulty, and adjacent-decoy errors. We build on these findings to examine how KG selection profiles change with relation availability and presentation, and which incomplete outputs persist or change on matched tasks. This extends the interpretation of the prior comparison rather than claiming the first KG transfer experiment.

Our analysis uses archived outcomes spanning seven task families and three language models. Raw versus Raw+E adds explicit relations to otherwise matched entity cards. Controlled triples, templated prose, and alternative card hosting retain designated unique facts while changing their presentation; both-card hosting additionally changes repetition and input length. Adjacent-context conditions introduce connected but query-irrelevant candidates. Separating these comparisons matters: supplying previously unavailable relations is different from presenting the same facts differently, and native descriptions are not assumed to contain every reference relation.

We ask three questions. \textbf{RQ1:} Which structural selection profiles reported for tools are also observed in KG entity selection, and where do the KG task-family and reference-size profiles differ? \textbf{RQ2:} How do relation availability, matched-fact presentation, and adjacent distractors condition these KG profiles? \textbf{RQ3:} Which residual output errors remain when relations are explicit, and how do they change on the same tasks under presentation and query-formulation variants? The Tool-side comparison uses previously reported findings; we do not estimate a new harmonized cross-domain effect. Family comparisons likewise do not isolate topology from query semantics and entity or relation types.

The study contributes an evidence-conditioned assessment of KG selection profiles in relation to prior Tool-to-KG findings. Its additional evidence consists of paired KG relation-availability and matched-fact presentation comparisons, together with localized analyses of incomplete and excessive selections and their transitions. Recall measures coverage of the exhaustive reference, while precision and exact recovery distinguish over-selection and complete-set correctness. The analysis reuses existing tasks and outcomes; exact task/response overlap with Appendix~O remains unquantified, and independent replication is not claimed.

The results show that relation disclosure increases coverage, while residual profiles vary across task families and models. On incoming-tree tasks, some high-precision outputs retain only part of the required route set. Presentation variants can correct these outputs but also introduce regressions; a decrease in one error signature need not produce a comparable increase in exact recovery. These findings qualify the interpretation of structural transfer: explicit relations enable stronger recovery in the studied KG conditions, but neither relation visibility nor a shared motif label is a sufficient explanation of complete selection behavior.
