\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Structural tool selection and prior Tool-to-KG evidence.}
Tool Graph Complexity distinguishes reference-set non-minimality from dependency composition and analyzes motif-associated selection difficulty~\cite{anonymousToolGraph}. Its tool-side analysis motivates studying structural profiles beyond reference count, without treating every raw motif difference as a size-adjusted effect. Appendix~O directly examines KG entity selection with motif and size variation, explicit relations, and adjacent decoys. Its Chain inversion, residual incoming-tree difficulty, and decoy-related errors are antecedent findings for the present study. We investigate the stability and limits of these profiles through KG relation-availability and matched-fact presentation contrasts and localized output-level diagnostics. Differences in task references, populations, prompts, and model protocols preclude interpreting cross-paper score differences as an isolated substrate effect; exact task/response reuse remains unquantified.

\paragraph{Entity retrieval and knowledge-grounded question answering.}
RoG organizes KG reasoning through relation-path planning, retrieval, and answer generation~\cite{luo2024rog}. STaRK evaluates retrieval of answer entities under textual and relational query requirements~\cite{wu2024stark}, while G-Retriever uses prize-collecting Steiner tree optimization to construct graph context for textual graph question answering~\cite{he2024gretriever}. These studies provide context for selecting relational information, but their evaluation targets differ from ours. We evaluate selection from a fixed catalogue that contains every reference entity, with an explicit requirement to recover all query-permitted route entities, including intermediate entities. We do not evaluate open-corpus retrieval or the correctness of generated answers. The underlying corporate graph is reused from Kirpichev et al.~\cite{kirpichev2026towards}; its construction is not a contribution of this study.

\paragraph{Graph structure, task comprehension, and representation.}
Talk like a Graph studies how graph encodings, task types, and structure affect language-model performance~\cite{fatemi2024talklikegraph}. Dai et al. investigate graph-pattern comprehension and discovery using terminological and topological descriptions~\cite{dai2025graphpatterns}. These works establish relevant axes of variation; neither using motifs nor observing representation sensitivity is itself our novelty claim. Our output is an exhaustive entity set for a specified query, rather than a graph-pattern label. We organize its recovery by task family and reference size while retaining the differences in query operations, direction, entity types, and relation types that accompany those families.

\paragraph{Relational evidence and residual selection errors.}
KG-prompt research examines input formats, triple organization, and noisy or incomplete subgraphs through answer-based evaluation~\cite{dai2024kgunderstanding}. GraphWalk studies tool-based KG navigation and reports incomplete exploration when graph context is supplied directly~\cite{ghandi2026graphwalk}. Our contribution is therefore not the general observation that encoding matters or that exploration may be incomplete. We distinguish the addition of explicit relations from the presentation of designated matched facts and inspect final selected sets and paired corrections and regressions. These sets are observable outcomes, not internal traversal traces. Disconnected/adjacent conditions further examine query-irrelevant neighboring candidates; adjacency alone does not define irrelevance. Recall, precision, and exact recovery describe exhaustive selection performance without identifying minimal sufficient card sets, downstream QA gains, or a common causal mechanism shared with tool selection.
