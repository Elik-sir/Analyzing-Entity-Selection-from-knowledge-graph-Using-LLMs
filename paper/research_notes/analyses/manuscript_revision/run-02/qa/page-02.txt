2 Related Work and Prior Transfer Evidence
Structural difficulty in tool selection. Tool Graph Complexity
separates reference-set minimality from dependency composition
and reports motif-associated differences in selection success [ 1].
Its size-adjusted sequential penalties should not be conflated with
every motif’s raw difficulty: the adjusted Tree-in interval includes
zero relative to Star-out. Appendix O already evaluates KG mo-
tifs, size, explicit relations, and adjacent decoys. Its Chain inver-
sion and residual incoming-tree interpretation are antecedent
findings, not discoveries claimed here. Our increment concerns
archived relation-availability and matched-fact presentation con-
trasts and more localized output-level diagnostics. Different refer-
ences, prompts, populations, and model protocols prevent direct
cross-paper score subtraction.
Graph representation and task comprehension. Talk like a Graph
establishes that graph encodings, task type, and structure affect
LLM performance [ 4]. Dai et al. investigate terminology- and
topology-based pattern comprehension and discovery [ 3]. Our
output is a set of entities covering all permitted query routes,
rather than a motif label or one discovered pattern. This task dis-
tinction does not make representation sensitivity itself novel.
KG evidence and traversal failures. KG-prompt research studies
input formats, triple organization, and noisy or incomplete evi-
dence, including answer-based evaluation [ 2]. GraphWalk studies
tool-mediated navigation and reports incomplete exploration in
graph-context settings [ 5]. Accordingly, we do not claim the first
observation of incomplete traversal. We measure final-set signa-
tures and changes on the same archived tasks; these are not exe-
cution traces. The corporate graph and earlier QA benchmark be-
long to Kirpichev et al. [ 6]; graph construction and downstream
QA gains are outside our contribution.
3 Task, Reference, and Relational Evidence
Source graph and historical scope. We reuse the corporate KG
snapshot described by Kirpichev et al. [ 6], covering S&P 500 com-
panies and associated sectors, industries, executives, investors,
products, resources, locations, and news. The authors confirm un-
changed snapshot reuse. We analyze the archived, frozen tasks
rather than reconstruct their historical database extraction from
current software. Appendix B reproduces the author-supplied
Cypher extraction specification. It defines fixed small instances
and an induced-edge filter; it is not presented as the complete exe-
cutable pipeline for all archived sizes or per-cell sampling quotas.
No database query was executed during this analysis.
Task families and questions. The Core panel has 100 base tasks
in each of seven families: chain, star-in, star-out, tree-in, tree-out,
rhombus, and hourglass. Five families have six entities; rhombi
have four and hourglasses five. Chain questions follow outgoing
routes until the first entity of a specified type on each route. Stars
ask for incoming or outgoing one-hop neighbours. Trees ask for
two-step incoming or outgoing traversal; rhombi use two-step out-
going traversal. A two-step route terminates early only if no fur-
ther unvisited neighbour is permitted. Hourglass questions request
incoming and outgoing one-hop neighbours in separate groups.
Question templates are instantiated using the anchor name/-
type, direction, and stopping rule. Step-by-step and direct formu-
lations express the same endpoint query; the former enumerates
the traversal operations. These families thus vary in topology and
query operation. In particular, outward trees and rhombi share the
outgoing two-step query, while other families use different direc-
tions or stopping rules. An unadjusted seven-family ranking is nei-
ther an equal-size nor a pure-topology comparison.
Candidates and distractors. Each main task presents 25 named
candidate entities, including the motif entities and distractors. Dis-
connected distractors have no edges to reference entities in either
direction, although they may connect to each other. In the adja-
cent variant, up to ten distractors are neighbours of motif entities;
remaining distractors are disconnected. Paired noise variants re-
tain the question and reference but may contain different distrac-
tor identities. Explicit evidence includes relations involving distrac-
tors, not only reference-induced edges.

For example, one archived rhombus has two branches from
the anchor through two intermediate entities to one endpoint. Its
endpoint answer has one entity and its reference four. Removing

indispensable information. The selection objective is complete
route coverage, not minimum sufficient evidence or final answer
correctness.
Evidence conditions. Raw presents original entity metadata;
Raw+E adds explicit relations to the same cards. Actual archived
inputs, questions, candidate order, and references match after re-
moving only the relation fields. This is an information-availability
contrast; native descriptions are not assumed to express all graph
relations. Controlled triples and templated prose instead encode
designated matched graph facts. Source-card, destination-card,
and both-card variants alter presentation location while retaining
relation direction. Names/types remain visible in all conditions.
