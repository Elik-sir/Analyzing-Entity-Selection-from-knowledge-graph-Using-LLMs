4 Experimental Design and Evaluation
Archived models and populations. We analyze Gemma-4-31B,
Qwen3.6-Flash, and Ministral-14B as identified in the archive.
Core has 700 base tasks, with 100 in each of seven families, under
13 conditions. Five families have six nodes; rhombus and hour-
glass retain four and five. Full Raw/Raw+E has 3,400 bases per
condition/model: chain sizes 3–6, stars 3–10, trees 5–10, rhombus
four, and hourglass five, totaling 34 motif-size cells of 100 tasks.
All main candidate pools contain 25 entities.
The 700 Core base IDs also occur in Full Raw/Raw+E. Models
and conditions reuse base tasks, so counts across panels are not
independent replications. Sizes are different frozen cohorts rather
than paired expansions of one graph. The separate additional-size
and incremental panels are supplementary and are not pooled into
the main comparisons.
Comparisons. RQ1 uses Core source-card triples with discon-
nected (K1) and adjacent (K2) distractors and the Full size grid.
RQ2 compares Raw with Raw+E, matched triples with text, and
hosting on source, destination, or both cards. K1, K3, and K4 retain
a procedural question; K7 uses a direct formulation of the same
endpoint query with source-card triples. K5 and K6 are the text
counterparts of disconnected and adjacent conditions; K8 uses text
with a direct query. Core K-conditions use pseudonyms. Archived
inputs establish pairing and fact invariants, independently of cur-
rent builder defaults.
Responses and evidence coverage. The main protocol requests
JSON with selected catalogue names and an insufficient-evidence
flag. Valid selections contain exact, unique candidate names; the
flag does not modify set scores. Available Qwen/Ministral records
specify OpenRouter, temperature zero, a 1,024-token limit, and
JSON-object formatting. Missing Gemma settings are not filled
from current defaults. Some controlled Gemma outcomes retain
cached individual metrics without original replies or selected iden-
tities; these support aggregate recall, precision, exact, and FP/FN
categories, but not identity-level route signatures. Full Raw/Raw+E
has stronger reply coverage. Missing and invalid outcomes remain
in planned denominators.

An empty selection receives zero recall and precision. Recall is
primary because the selection target requires complete route cov-
erage. Precision measures excess selection; exact recovery is one
only when 𝑆 = 𝑊 . We report macro means of individual scores
over all planned outcomes, with invalid/missing outcomes con-
tributing zero. Thus scores also reflect protocol reliability; they are
not pooled micro ratios or valid-only summaries. Reference cov-
erage is distinct from endpoint-answer correctness and minimal
sufficient evidence.
Analysis and uncertainty scope. The figures and tables use saved
aggregate scores and existing bounded selection diagnostics; this
revision launches no model, judge, or database calls. Core condi-
tion contrasts match 700 bases per model; Raw/Raw+E matches
3,400. Differences are right-minus-left macro means on these com-
plete paired populations. Source-row mappings reproduce the
existing paired export. Recall/precision profiles and contrasts are
descriptive: no new confidence intervals, significance claims, or
motif-by-condition tests are introduced. Earlier uncertainty esti-
mates cover F1/exact, and are not transferred to recall. Repeated
anchors and shared entities further restrict population inference.
5 Results
5.1 Structural Profiles: Motif and Size
Figure 1 shows task-family differences at a common reference size.
Under K1, six-node incoming-tree recall is 0.972 for Gemma, 0.603
for Qwen, and 0.853 for Ministral. Outward stars are close to com-
plete coverage. The common size does not eliminate differences in
query semantics, relation types, or sampled entities; it rules out
a node-count-only description of these observed task profiles, not
every competing explanation.
Precision supplies a different view. Qwen’s K1 incoming-tree
precision is 0.988, despite recall of 0.603 and exact recovery of 0.150.
It often selects a small, mostly reference-consistent set while fail-
ing to cover the reference. Gemma’s corresponding precision is
1.000 and exact 0.870. Appendix A reports precision profiles sepa-
rately rather than combining the two quantities into one F1 curve.
Size sensitivity also depends on family and model (Figure 2).
Under Raw+E, chain recall changes from 0.990 at three nodes
to 0.790 at six for Qwen, and from 0.927 to 0.595 for Ministral.
Gemma changes from 0.990 to 1.000. Outward stars provide con-
trary profiles: Qwen changes from 0.997 at three nodes to 0.998 at
ten, Gemma remains at 1.000, and Ministral changes from 0.870 to
0.950. These comparisons describe different accepted cohorts, not
beneficial or harmful effects of adding a node to the same task.
Incoming-tree Raw+E recall is lower at ten than five nodes for
all three models: 0.910 to 0.841, 0.624 to 0.375, and 0.540 to 0.403
for Gemma, Qwen, and Ministral. Intermediate values need not be
monotonic. The fixed candidate pool also changes the proportion
of reference entities as size increases. We therefore report the full
profiles, including exceptions, rather than treating node count as
an intrinsic complexity coefficient.
5.2 Relation Availability and Matched-Fact
Presentation
Explicit relation disclosure substantially increases recall (Table 1).
Raw/Raw+E mean recall changes from 0.198 to 0.972 for Gemma,
0.278 to 0.798 for Qwen, and 0.218 to 0.785 for Ministral. The gains
are 0.774, 0.519, and 0.567. Precision changes from 0.859 to 0.988,
0.885 to 0.975, and 0.459 to 0.976, respectively. High Raw precision
can therefore coexist with low route coverage. Since native meta-
data is not verified sufficient for every route, low Raw recall is not
automatically a failure to reason from available facts.
