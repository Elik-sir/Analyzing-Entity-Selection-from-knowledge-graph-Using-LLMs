From Tool Selection to Knowledge-Graph Entity Selection:
Structural Profiles and Relational Evidence
Anonymous Author(s)
Abstract
Tool-selection success varies with the pattern of dependencies
between tools, motivating questions about which structural diffi-
culties persist when relations are explicitly supplied. We revisit
this problem through archived knowledge-graph entity selection
on seven task families and three language models. Building on a
prior Tool-to-KG study, we distinguish adding relational evidence
from changing the presentation of matched facts and introduc-
ing adjacent distractors. Recall is the primary metric for recovery
of the entity-route reference, with precision and exact recovery
providing complementary diagnostics. Adding relations increases
mean recall by 0.774, 0.519, and 0.567 for Gemma, Qwen, and
Ministral. Residual profiles depend on task family, size, and model.
On six-node incoming trees, Qwen recall is 0.603 with source-card
relations and 0.802 when the same facts appear on both endpoint
cards; moving them to destination cards alone leaves recall at 0.603.
Output-level analyses retain both corrections and regressions un-
der presentation changes. The findings distinguish relation avail-
ability from complete route recovery and refine hypotheses about
structural selection errors without asserting a universal topology
ranking or shared causal mechanism.

1 Introduction
Selecting the right elements from a catalogue is a prerequisite for
many language-model agents that access Web information. A tool-
using agent must retain the operations needed to satisfy a request;
a knowledge-grounded system must retain the entities that con-
nect a question to its supporting facts. In either setting, choos-
ing individually plausible names can omit intermediate context,
while selecting too broadly can introduce irrelevant elements. Un-
derstanding selection reliability therefore requires examining how
the requested elements compose.
Tool Graph Complexity reports that tool-selection success dif-
fers across dependency patterns, beyond what the number of ref-
erence tools alone describes [ 1]. Its controlled analysis retains a

The same work investigates this question using a knowledge
graph (KG) in Appendix O. It reports partial structural transfer, an
easier explicit-KG Chain profile, residual incoming-tree difficulty,
and adjacent-decoy errors [ 1]. These antecedent observations mo-
tivate examining which errors remain after relation disclosure and
how evidence conditions constrain their interpretation.
We address this question through an archived corporate-KG
entity-selection study. The graph connects companies, people,
products, locations, and news and was developed for corporate
GraphRAG evaluation [ 6]. Our Web-related concern is the reli-
ability of selecting connected evidence for querying interlinked
corporate information: a route can be incomplete even when plau-
sible endpoint entities are retained. We evaluate recovery of a
declared entity-route reference, separating this access step from
final answer correctness.
Our analysis distinguishes three comparisons. Raw versus
Raw+E adds explicit relations to otherwise matched entity cards.
Controlled triples versus templated prose and alternative card
hosting retain designated facts while changing their presentation.
Adjacent-context pairs add connected but query-irrelevant distrac-
tors. Keeping these contrasts separate matters: an information gain
cannot be interpreted as merely an encoding effect, and complete
route coverage is not the same objective as finding one sufficient
answer.
We ask: RQ1, how do entity-selection profiles vary with task
family and reference size? RQ2, how do relation availability, fact
presentation, and adjacent contexts change recall and residual er-
rors? RQ3, what do these observations imply for the interpretation
of Tool-to-KG structural transfer? RQ3 is a descriptive comparison
with prior evidence, not a new matched cross-domain experiment.
The additional contribution is a diagnostic follow-up: (i) sepa-
rating relation disclosure from fact-preserving presentation, and
(ii) localizing residual omissions, excess selection, and same-task
presentation sensitivity. Family and size profiles provide its struc-
tural context. Recall is primary; precision and exact distinguish se-
lectivity and complete-set recovery. This is an empirical analysis
of an existing KG, without a new algorithm or resource claim. Ex-
act task/response overlap with Appendix O is unquantified; we do
not claim independent replication.
The results show why relation visibility is insufficient as a
complete explanation. Disclosure substantially increases recall,
yet residual profiles differ across families and models. On six-
node incoming trees, Qwen retains high precision but incomplete
recall; repeating the same relations across both endpoint cards
increases recall more than moving them to destination cards alone.
