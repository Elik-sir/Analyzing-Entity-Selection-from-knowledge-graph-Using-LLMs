71 of 100 K1 incoming-tree outputs equal the anchor plus its first
incoming neighbourhood. Under K4, 38 of those 71 become exact,
while six of the 15 initially exact outputs lose exactness. Under

selected identities needed for this particular check.
5.3 Adjacent Contexts
Adjacent-minus-disconnected recall differences are negative for all
three models in both triples and text contrasts (Table 1). The mo-
tif profiles show where coverage remains incomplete under these
conditions (Figure 1). Distractor identities and graph density also
change in the paired noise variants. We therefore interpret these as
archived context contrasts, not isolated causal effects of adjacency
or evidence of a universal motif-by-noise interaction.
6 Transfer Implications and Limitations
What carries across the two selection settings? The Tool Selec-
tion premise is that dependency pattern matters beyond reference
count [1]. KG profiles likewise vary across task families at common
sizes, and the size gradient differs by family. The shared descrip-
tive lesson is to evaluate structured selection by task family and
evidence condition. A universal ranking is not supported: prior Ap-
pendix O already documents partial transfer and Chain inversion,
and our explicit/native profiles further depend on presentation and
model. We use Tool-side findings as literature evidence rather than
a new harmonized Tool arm.
Tool dependency edges and factual KG relations have different
semantics. Tool compatibility does not establish that every pre-
decessor is mandatory; execution additionally requires argument
bindings and order. KG incoming-route union is not automatically
a conjunctive tool-input join. The source paper’s raw Tree-in diffi-
culty also differs from its size-adjusted, null-compatible coefficient.
Consequently, residual KG incoming-tree errors do not indepen-
dently validate a common Tree-in mechanism in Tool Selection.
Four distinguishable explanations, not established causes. Depen-
dency inference is compatible with the prior easing of explicit
KG chains, but Raw/Raw+E supplies potentially absent informa-
tion and is not an equally informative implicit-versus-explicit
tool experiment. Route/branch coverage is compatible with high-
precision incomplete selections and frequent first-neighbourhood
sets. In the inspected Qwen K1 incoming-tree slice, first-level re-
tention is much stronger than endpoint retention; “choosing one
whole branch” is not an adequate universal description. Query-
constrained traversal concerns direction, depth, and stopping rules.
Distractor discrimination concerns the rejection of connected but
query-irrelevant entities; the adjacent-context recall contrasts mo-
tivate examining this operation. These operations can fail together,
and final sets cannot reveal their internal execution order.
Both-card hosting motivates a hypothesis about access to dis-
tributed evidence, but also changes repetition, salience, and length.
The contrary destination-hosting results and remaining errors con-
strain an easy repair claim. Testing the dependency-inference ex-
planation directly would require the same real tool items with and
without a complete candidate-dependency overlay; separating
hosting from repetition would require matched-length controls.
These are future discriminating tests, not experiments reported
here.
What this benchmark identifies. We score the union of allowed
route vertices, including the anchor. This is distinct from minimal

answers. A smaller selection may suffice for an endpoint answer
