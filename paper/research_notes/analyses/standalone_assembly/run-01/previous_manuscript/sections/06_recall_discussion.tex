\section{Transfer Implications and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{What carries across the two selection settings?}
The Tool Selection premise is that dependency pattern matters beyond reference count~\cite{anonymousToolGraph}. KG profiles likewise vary across task families at common sizes, and the size gradient differs by family. The shared descriptive lesson is to evaluate structured selection by task family and evidence condition. A universal ranking is not supported: prior Appendix~O already documents partial transfer and Chain inversion, and our explicit/native profiles further depend on presentation and model. We use Tool-side findings as literature evidence rather than a new harmonized Tool arm.

Tool dependency edges and factual KG relations have different semantics. Tool compatibility does not establish that every predecessor is mandatory; execution additionally requires argument bindings and order. KG incoming-route union is not automatically a conjunctive tool-input join. The source paper's raw Tree-in difficulty also differs from its size-adjusted, null-compatible coefficient. Consequently, residual KG incoming-tree errors do not independently validate a common Tree-in mechanism in Tool Selection.

\paragraph{Four distinguishable explanations, not established causes.}
\emph{Dependency inference} is compatible with the prior easing of explicit KG chains, but Raw/Raw+E supplies potentially absent information and is not an equally informative implicit-versus-explicit tool experiment. \emph{Route/branch coverage} is compatible with high-precision incomplete selections and frequent first-neighbourhood sets. In the inspected Qwen K1 incoming-tree slice, first-level retention is much stronger than endpoint retention; ``choosing one whole branch'' is not an adequate universal description. \emph{Query-constrained traversal} concerns direction, depth, and stopping rules. \emph{Distractor discrimination} concerns the rejection of connected but query-irrelevant entities; the adjacent-context recall contrasts motivate examining this operation. These operations can fail together, and final sets cannot reveal their internal execution order.

Both-card hosting motivates a hypothesis about access to distributed evidence, but also changes repetition, salience, and length. The contrary destination-hosting results and remaining errors constrain an easy repair claim. Testing the dependency-inference explanation directly would require the same real tool items with and without a complete candidate-dependency overlay; separating hosting from repetition would require matched-length controls. These are future discriminating tests, not experiments reported here.

\paragraph{What this benchmark identifies.}
We score the union of allowed route vertices, including the anchor. This is distinct from minimal graph support, indispensable visible cards, and correct endpoint answers. A smaller selection may suffice for an endpoint answer even when it fails the route-coverage convention. High recall alone also permits selecting everything, which has recall one by construction; precision and exact are therefore necessary companion diagnostics.

Some rule violations are observationally indistinguishable in simple contexts. On the 100 inspected Qwen K1 six-node incoming-tree inputs, ignoring direction or increasing the depth bound from two to three produces the same coverage set as the correct rule. Correct final sets in that slice cannot establish adherence to those rules. Adjacent contexts can supply distinguishing cases, but different rules have different diagnostic support. A full anchor-connected-component baseline has not been measured; neither ideal performance by that heuristic nor the absence of shortcuts is claimed.

\paragraph{Scope, provenance, and originality.}
Families also differ in query semantics, relation/type distributions, and accepted entities. Sizes use different sampled cohorts; fixed catalogue size changes target prevalence and distractor count. The study covers one corporate KG and frozen model/protocol labels, with no generation-seed replication or new KG evaluation. Base tasks, anchors, and reference entities overlap; descriptive averages do not remove these dependencies. We introduce no recall/precision significance or population-causal claims.

The graph is reused~\cite{kirpichev2026towards}, and the related KG line already appears in Appendix~O~\cite{anonymousToolGraph}. Exact cross-paper row overlap remains unquantified. The additional scope is the evidence-availability and matched-fact analysis with localized residual-error interpretation; it is not a newly constructed benchmark or independent replication.

Frozen-reference checks establish consistency of the presented tasks, not completeness against the unloaded source database. Missing Gemma replies/settings, native sufficiency, and incremental histories remain limitations. Saved rows, selectors, denominators, hashes, and plotting scripts support reproduction of these summaries; resource redistribution and graph access are separate matters.

\paragraph{Responsibility and downstream scope.}
This study does not evaluate a new human-subject population or establish downstream QA improvement. Generative AI assistance supported evidence organization, plotting code, and manuscript drafting; experimental values derive from saved outcomes. Authors remain responsible for source verification, anonymity, resource permissions, and the relation to prior work.
